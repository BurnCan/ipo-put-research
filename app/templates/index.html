What this hypothesis means

We are testing whether recent IPOs with both strong positive price momentum and high realized volatility shortly before their primary lockup expiration are more vulnerable to a subsequent decline. The bearish mechanism under study is that an extended or strong-performing but unstable recent IPO may be especially vulnerable when lockup expiration makes additional shares eligible for sale—a potential supply catalyst, not evidence that holders actually sell.

Momentum alone is not the hypothesis. Volatility alone is not the hypothesis. The hypothesis is their interaction around the supply catalyst. These are research signals: they do not claim that every lockup creates selling pressure or that every high_high observation will decline. The thresholds were frozen before prospective validation and must not change in response to later observations.

How the test works

  1. Observe at T-5.Five canonical XNYS trading sessions before the lockup event.
  2. Measure one canonical window.return_20d is the stock return over the exact 20-session interval ending at T-5 and requires 21 canonical closing-price observations. realized_vol_20d is trailing realized volatility over that same feature window.
  3. Apply the frozen interaction rule.A high_high target requires both features to exceed the registry-supplied frozen thresholds shown above.
  4. Observe the outcome.The frozen outcome is the stock return at +20 trading sessions after the lockup event.

Evidence roles: M7 historical discovery is not out-of-sample performance. Strict M8 is the primary prospective evidence. Shadow prospective is secondary evidence and never enters primary M8 results.
